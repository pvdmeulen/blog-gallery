#### June 2021, Dalby, Sweden

My mum walked into the living room, a few days after _that day_, holding stacks of old photos. You probably know the kind - paper packs filled with photos developed at a local shop. As she sat down and went through them, it was like a small window opened up: a window into a life before me or my brother, when it was just my dad and my mum living life together. I’d heard some stories, of course, and we still have some family friends from my parents' time in university, but I hadn’t seen many photos. There, on the living room table, were dozens (if not hundreds) of photos sprawled out.

{{< ps-gallery folder="/papa/" >}}

The majority were photos from their many holidays around Europe. They had a real passion for cycling and camping, something which continued into my childhood, and my brother's childhood, as well.

{{< ps-gallery folder="/papa2/" >}}

When me and my brother arrived, my mum kept taking photos. As a kid growing up, I probably found it very annoying (sorry mum), but now I realise the value in taking photos. Yes, photos of the big moments, nice holidays, important events - but also photos of the everyday, the mundane. It's those days with my dad I miss the most: hearing him play guitar on a quiet Sunday morning, watching recorded episodes of Star Trek together every Wednesday, or playing chess together (and him beating me every time).

{{< ps-gallery folder="/papa3/" >}}

The photos I'm taking won't be nearly as nostalgic (not for a long time, anyway), and probably not as good, but it's all for a good cause: being able to look back at memories with my wife, my brother, mum, and friends.
